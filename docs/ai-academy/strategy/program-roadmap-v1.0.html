1 · Market opportunity & thesis

The gap. Per NASSCOM-Deloitte "Advancing India's AI Skills", India's AI talent demand grows from ~600,000–650,000 (2022) to over 1.25 million by 2027 (~15% CAGR) while the AI software/services market grows 25–35% CAGR. Bain frames it as ~2.3M openings vs ~1.2M talent — a 1.5–2× shortfall.

The thesis. Colleges produce graduates with theory but not production AI skills. The 2026 market hires for the ability to ship and evaluate AI systems under cost/latency/safety constraints. A structured, NSQF-aligned, project-heavy academy converts that gap into placed talent — monetised through hire-train-deploy (employer-funded), B2B corporate upskilling, and government-subsidised cohorts.

Rail 4 — State missions, CSR & campus

State AI/skilling missions (Telangana is notably active), CSR funds from IT employers (skilling is a permitted CSR head), and AICTE/university MoUs for embedded final-year programs. These are the fastest to close and least bureaucratic — use them for your first cohorts while Rails 1–2 process.

Sequencing recommendation: Rail 4 (fast pilot cohort) → Rail 1 (NSQF-align + FSP listing) → Rail 2 (NSDC/SMART accreditation) → Rail 3 (IndiaAI partnership). Rails 1–2 both require NSQF/NOS alignment, so do §7.2 first — it is the shared unlock.
